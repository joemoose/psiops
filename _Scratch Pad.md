# Forked Choices

**Source**: GRASP by Richard Kelly, CC BY 3.0.

If the dice feel too random to your group, or if your players see 7–9 results as failures instead of partial successes, you may want to implement Forked Choices.

With Forked Choices, on a 7--9, you don't tell a player their complication. You give them two (or more) potential complications and ask them which happens.

For example, Fred throws a lit dynamite stick at a parasitic stingray man. Fred's player rolls a 7. As the Referee, you offer "either the dynamite misses and lands in the water, or it deals full damage but also takes out the boardwalk beneath you, and you plunge into the sea." Fred's player chooses to take out the stingray man and the boardwalk, and now Fred is treading water, surrounded by wooden debris, and hoping there's nothing else hostile beneath the surface.

Implementing Forked Choices requires the Referee to invest a little more effort, as you'll need to devise several possible outcomes for every complicated roll. However, this additional work significantly enhances player agency, making it a worthwhile endeavor.

## FIST Ammo 

Abstract ammo tracking Ranged weapons have a new stat called AMMO, set to 6 at the beginning of each mission. After attacking with a ranged weapon, roll 1D6:

- If your roll was lower than the AMMO score, decrease the weapon’s AMMO score by 1.
- If your roll exceeds the AMMO score, the weapon is out of ammo and cannot be used until it's refilled (0 AMMO).
- If the roll was equal, keep AMMO the same.

Finding more ammo for a weapon increases its score by 1, and choosing RESTOCK during a rendezvous can refill one weapon’s AMMO to 6 instead of refilling a limited-use item.

TIME AND ACTIONS

A combat round represents 6 seconds of in-game time. During this, characters may move and take one Action. An Action may be an attack, moving again, using a psionic talent, or any similar act taking up to 6 seconds.

Interrupting Actions: It is possible for a character to hold their action. They must specify under what conditions they will act. For example, “I hold my action until I see an enemy come around the corner.” When the conditions are met, the character may act instantly, interrupting the target’s action. 

Movement: In-combat movement is abstracted for purposes of determining ranges to targets. However, in some cases it is useful to have a more concrete measurement. When a character spends an Action moving on foot, they move around 10m.

Falling prone requires no Action and may be done freely on the character’s turn; getting up from a prone position takes one Action.

Attacks of Opportunity: Should a combatant engaged in Close range attempt to move out of combat, their opponent may make a free melee attack if they have a melee weapon at the ready. To safely disengage from Close range, a combatant must spend one action to Disengage, and then they may make a normal movement action without risk.

Attacking: Roll the Combat skill to hit a target with a melee weapon at Close range, to hit a target with a handgun at Close or Near range, or to hit a target with a rifle at Near or Far range. See the Range Table below.

RANGES

Close: Up to 2 meters away. Melee range. A character may move this distance without using any Combat Actions.

Near: Pistol range. A character may move anywhere within this distance by expanding one Combat Action.

Far: Rifle range. A character must expend between 2 and 4 Combat Actions to traverse this distance, at the Referee’s discretion.

Distant: Beyond rifle range. A character usually cannot traverse such a distance within the short time span of personal combat.
