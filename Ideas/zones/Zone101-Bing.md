# Zone101 - Bing

**Short version first:**  
You can absolutely replace your linear **range bands** with a **zone-based tactical engine** that preserves OSR grit, keeps firefights fast, and still gives players meaningful spatial decisions. Below is a **complete, immediately usable framework** you can drop into PSIOPS or any modern‑military RPG. It includes: what zones *are*, how to build them, how to run them, how to handle movement, cover, suppression, and how to convert your old weapon‑range logic (advantage/disadvantage/normal/impossible) into a zone‑based matrix.

# 🎯 Core Takeaway  
A **zone system** treats the battlefield as a handful of meaningful areas—each with its own tactical properties—rather than a grid or a linear strip. Characters move between zones, not inches. Ranges become **relationships between zones**, not measurements. This preserves tactical clarity while keeping the game fast, gritty, and OSR‑lean.

---

# 🧭 What Zones *Are* (and Are Not)

Zones are **discrete chunks of space** that matter tactically. They are not distances; they are **areas of influence**.

A zone is defined by:
- **What it contains** (cover, vantage, hazards)
- **How it connects** to other zones
- **How hard it is to move into/out of**

Think of zones as the answer to:  
**"What parts of this scene matter tactically?"**  
not  
**"Where exactly is everyone standing?"**

Examples of zones in a modern firefight:
- **Streetfront** — cars, dumpsters, low cover  
- **Upper Floor** — elevation, windows, overwatch  
- **Back Alley** — concealment, flanking route  
- **Lobby** — choke point, hard cover  
- **Rooftop** — long sightlines, exposed  

Zones are **not**:
- 5‑ft squares  
- exact distances  
- linear range bands  
- simulationist geometry  

---

# 🧩 How Many Zones?  
For gritty, OSR‑adjacent modern combat, the sweet spot is:

**3–7 zones per scene.**

Fewer = too abstract  
More = too fiddly  

---

# 🛠️ How to Build Zones (Fast)

When a scene starts, the GM identifies:
1. **Where the action can happen**  
2. **Where people can move**  
3. **Where cover exists**  
4. **Where sightlines matter**

Then draw a quick **zone map** (mentally or on paper):

```
[Streetfront] — [Lobby] — [Stairwell] — [Upper Floor]
       \                          /
        \— [Back Alley] — [Rooftop]
```

Each connection is a **movement path**.

---

# 🚶 Movement Rules (Simple + Tactical)

Movement is **one zone per action** by default.

Modifiers:
- **Crossing fire**: entering a zone under active fire requires a test or you stop short.  
- **Difficult terrain**: rubble, crowds, smoke = movement costs 2 actions.  
- **Free transitions**: open doors, same building interior, etc.

Optional OSR‑style grit:
- **Dash**: move 2 zones but suffer disadvantage on defense until next turn.  
- **Crawl**: move 1 zone with advantage on stealth/cover.

# 🔫 Weapon Ranges in a Zone System  
Here's the part you asked for: converting your **advantage / disadvantage / normal / impossible** logic into **zone relationships**.

## Step 1 — Define Range Categories  
Use four simple categories:

- **Close** (same zone)  
- **Near** (adjacent zone)  
- **Far** (2 zones away)  
- **Distant** (3+ zones away or no direct line)

These are *not* distances—they're **zone relationships**.

## Step 2 — Assign Each Weapon a Range Profile  
Example profiles for a modern game:

| Weapon | Close | Near | Far | Distant |
|-------|-------|------|-----|---------|
| **Pistol** | Advantage | Normal | Disadvantage | Impossible |
| **SMG** | Advantage | Normal | Disadvantage | Impossible |
| **Shotgun** | Advantage | Normal | Disadvantage | Impossible |
| **Carbine** | Normal | Advantage | Normal | Disadvantage |
| **Assault Rifle** | Disadvantage | Advantage | Advantage | Normal |
| **DMR** | Disadvantage | Normal | Advantage | Advantage |
| **Sniper Rifle** | Impossible | Disadvantage | Normal | Advantage |
| **LMG** | Disadvantage | Normal | Advantage | Advantage |

You can tune this to taste.

# 🛡️ Cover, Concealment, and Suppression

Zones shine here because each zone can have **properties**.

## Zone Properties  
- **Light Cover**: +1 defense  
- **Heavy Cover**: +2 defense  
- **Concealment**: attacks suffer disadvantage  
- **Exposed**: attackers gain advantage  
- **Elevated**: advantage vs targets in lower zones  
- **Choke Point**: entering requires a test  
- **Hazardous**: moving through causes damage or tests  

These are **attached to the zone**, not the character.

## Suppression  
If a character lays down suppressive fire into a zone:
- Everyone in that zone must test or become **Pinned**  
- Moving out of a suppressed zone requires a test  
- Entering a suppressed zone is **disadvantage**  

This makes suppression meaningful without grids.

# 🎮 Switching Between Narrative & Tactical Modes  
Your game wants to freely shift between:
- **Narrative mode** (loose, descriptive)  
- **Combat mode** (structured, tactical)  

Use this rule:

> **When precise spatial relationships matter, drop into zones.  
> When they stop mattering, collapse back to narrative.**

Example:
- Players approach a warehouse → narrative  
- Sniper fires from rooftop → switch to zones  
- Fight ends → collapse back to narrative  

Zones are a **temporary tactical overlay**, not a permanent map.

# 🧪 Practical Example: PSIOPS Firefight  
Scene: Abandoned factory.

Zones:
- **Loading Dock** — heavy cover  
- **Main Floor** — open, exposed  
- **Catwalk** — elevation, light cover  
- **Office Block** — choke point  
- **Exterior Yard** — concealment  

Connections:
- Yard ↔ Loading Dock ↔ Main Floor ↔ Office Block  
- Main Floor ↔ Catwalk  

A pistol user in the Main Floor shooting at someone in the Catwalk is at **Far** range (2 zones) → Disadvantage.

A sniper in the Yard shooting into the Catwalk is **Distant** → Advantage.

# 🧱 Why Zones Work Better Than Range Bands  
Range bands are **linear**.  
Zones are **relational**.

Range bands answer:  
**"How far apart are we?"**

Zones answer:  
**"What matters in this space?"**

Zones let you model:
- flanking  
- elevation  
- cover  
- suppression  
- movement choices  
- tactical geography  

…without ever touching a ruler.

- Close = same zone  
- Near = adjacent  
- Far = 2 zones  
- Distant = 3+ zones

Zone-based bonuses and penalties.

Narrative ↔ Tactical as needed.
