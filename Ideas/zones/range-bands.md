# Range Bands

> I've forgotten the TTRPG game I read that introduced an abstract method for tracking distance between opponents in combat, using a linear representation with different range bands. Opponents moved from one band to another. If you were in the same band, it was hand-to-hand combat; otherwise, it was a ranged attack between bands. The more bands between, the farther apart. It was abstract, essentially putting opponents on a segmented strip to measure distances (more theater of the mind) rather than using precise measurements, as with tabletop miniatures. Any idea of what TTRPG systems use this approach? Was likely fantasy, indie, OSR. But I don't recall the name.

## Most Likely: *The One Ring* / *Adventures in Middle-earth*

Probably the closest match to your description.

Combat uses a **stance line** and abstract engagement structure rather than grids. Distances are represented more conceptually than geometrically. Characters occupy positions relative to the fight rather than exact coordinates.

Key features:

* Engagement bands rather than measured movement
* Shared "close" positions for melee
* Rearward positions for ranged attacks
* Theater-of-the-mind friendly
* Linear-ish combat arrangement

Especially if you remember:

* A "front line" feeling
* Tactical positioning without maps
* Fantasy travel/adventure focus

---

## Very Strong Possibility: *Index Card RPG (ICRPG)*

Index Card RPG

ICRPG heavily popularized simplified range categories:

* **Close**
* **Near**
* **Far**

Movement shifts you between bands rather than counting squares.

It is:

* OSR-adjacent
* Indie fantasy
* Very abstract
* Often represented visually as simple spatial chunks

However, it usually uses *zones* more than a literal segmented strip.

---

## Also Plausible: *13th Age*

13th Age

Uses:

* Engaged
* Nearby
* Far away

Combat is intentionally abstract and anti-grid. Distances are conceptual, and melee requires engagement/shared proximity.

It does not usually use a literal "strip," but many GMs draw it that way informally.

## Other Systems with Similar Approaches

### *Fate Core*

Fate Core

Uses **zones**:

* Same zone = melee possible
* Adjacent zones = easy movement/ranged
* More zones = longer range

Very narrative and abstract.

---

### *Forbidden Lands*

Forbidden Lands

Uses range bands:

* Arm's Length
* Near
* Short
* Long

Movement shifts between bands rather than measured inches/hexes.

---

### *Shadow of the Demon Lord*

Shadow of the Demon Lord

Uses abstract distance categories:

* Immediate
* Short
* Medium
* Long
* Extreme

No strict gridded combat required.

---

### *Dragonbane*

Dragonbane

Also uses abstract movement/range categories in a lightweight fashion.
