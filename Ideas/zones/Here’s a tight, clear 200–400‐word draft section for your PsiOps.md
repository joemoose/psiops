

### Modes of Play: Narrative vs. Combat

PsiOps operates in two primary modes of play: **Narrative Mode** and **Combat Mode**. Each mode represents a different scale of action and uses different tools to portray time, distance, and decision‑making.

**Narrative Mode** covers the broad flow of the story—travel, investigation, planning, social interaction, and problem‑solving. Time and distance are treated flexibly and abstractly. Minutes, hours, or even days may pass in a single exchange, depending on what matters to the story. The GM doesn’t track exact movement rates or precise distances unless they become relevant. If the team needs to cross a forest, stake out a building, or wait for sunrise, the transition simply happens unless something interesting interrupts it. Narrative Mode emphasizes intent, choices, and outcomes rather than step‑by‑step procedure.

**Combat Mode** begins the moment the situation demands tactical clarity—when bullets fly, blades flash, or timing becomes critical. The game shifts to structured turns, defined zones, and clearly sequenced actions. Time becomes granular, measured in short rounds. Distance becomes concrete, represented by zones that determine who can reach whom, what can be targeted, and how the battlefield shifts. Players declare specific actions, resolve them in order, and react to rapidly changing threats. Combat Mode emphasizes positioning, timing, and tactical decision‑making.

The key difference between the two modes is **how precisely the game models time and space**. Narrative Mode zooms out to keep the story moving; Combat Mode zooms in to handle danger with clarity and fairness. The GM will signal when the game shifts modes, and players should adjust their thinking accordingly—from broad intentions to specific tactical choices.
