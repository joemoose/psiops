# GM Guide — Running Tactical Zones in PsiOps

## Purpose of the System

The Tactical Zone system exists to:
- preserve tactical decision-making,
- support theater-of-the-mind play,
- eliminate measurement and grid management,
- maintain modern military combat dynamics,
- and keep combat moving quickly.

Zones should create:
- meaningful movement,
- positional pressure,
- terrain advantages,
- and difficult tactical choices.

They should never become hidden geometry.

---

# 1. When to Enter Tactical Mode

Remain in operational narrative mode until:
- line-of-fire matters,
- multiple combatants maneuver simultaneously,
- exact tactical positioning affects outcomes,
- or the scene becomes chaotic enough to require structure.

Transition quickly:
1. Name the zones
2. Explain connections
3. State important traits
4. Place combatants

This should take under one minute.

---

# 2. Building Good Zones

## The Core Question

Ask:

> “What positions matter tactically in this scene?”

Not:
> “How do I divide the map?”

Zones are tactical ideas, not physical measurements.

---

## Recommended Zone Count

| Scene | Zones |
|---|---|
| Small interior | 2–3 |
| Building assault | 3–5 |
| Urban firefight | 4–6 |
| Large paranormal complex | 5–7 |

More than 7 usually recreates grid combat poorly.

---

## Good Zone Design

Every zone should imply:
- an advantage,
- a danger,
- a tradeoff,
- or a tactical opportunity.

If two zones feel identical:
- merge them.

---

## Strong Zone Names

Avoid abstract labels:
- “Zone A”
- “Sector 2”

Prefer tactical identity:
- “Collapsed Sanctuary”
- “Burned Motor Pool”
- “Observation Catwalk”
- “Kill Corridor”
- “Containment Chamber”

Names communicate tactics immediately.

---

# 3. Zone Connections

Zones form a network, not a line.

Example:

```text
          [Catwalk]
              |
[Garage] — [Factory Floor] — [Offices]
              |
         [Loading Yard]
```

Connections define:
- movement,
- engagement range,
- chokepoints,
- and flanking routes.

Do not overcomplicate adjacency.

If movement between two areas would reasonably take only moments under combat conditions, they are probably adjacent.

---

# 4. Tactical Terrain & Zone Traits

Traits define how a zone changes combat.

## Common Traits

| Trait | Effect |
|---|---|
| Heavy Cover | Harder to hit targets inside |
| Exposed | Easier to target occupants |
| Elevated | Better sightlines |
| Chokepoint | Restricts movement |
| Difficult Terrain | Costs extra movement |
| Hazardous | Causes damage or stress |
| Obscured | Visibility penalties |
| Psychic Distortion | Interferes with abilities |

---

## PsiOps-Specific Examples

### Psychic Bleed Zone
Residual psychic contamination:
- concentration tests suffer penalties,
- hallucinations possible,
- operators risk exposure stress.

### Reality Instability
Teleportation, clairvoyance, or remote perception may misfire.

### Whisper Zone
Sub-audible psychic noise:
- radio communication degraded,
- command coordination impaired.

### Echo Site
Paranormal resonance:
- psychic abilities become stronger,
- but anomalies become more aggressive.

---

# 5. Movement Pressure

Movement should create risk.

Examples:
- crossing a street under fire,
- moving through a burning laboratory,
- climbing toward an entrenched sniper,
- advancing through psychic interference.

Movement is where tactical decisions emerge.

---

## Useful Movement Conditions

### Crossing Fire
Entering the zone requires:
- suppression test,
- morale check,
- or defensive roll.

Failure may:
- halt movement,
- pin the operator,
- or force retreat.

---

### Difficult Terrain
Mud, rubble, crowds, psychic static:
- movement costs 2 actions,
- sprinting may be impossible.

---

### Free Transitions
Closely connected interiors may not require meaningful movement:
- adjacent office rooms,
- connected trench segments,
- same apartment interior.

Do not charge actions for movement that adds no tactical value.

---

# 6. Range and Weapons

Range emerges from adjacency.

| Distance | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Engaged | Same zone |
| Nearby | Adjacent |
| Far | Two zones |
| Distant | Three zones |
| Extreme | Four+ zones |

---

## Example Weapon Logic

| Weapon | Engaged | Nearby | Far | Distant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shotgun | Strong | Effective | Weak | Impossible |
| SMG | Strong | Effective | Weak | Impossible |
| Assault Rifle | Weak | Effective | Effective | Limited |
| Sniper Rifle | Impossible | Weak | Strong | Strong |
| Sidearm | Effective | Limited | Weak | Impossible |

The exact modifiers depend on the core resolution system.

The important part:
- weapons should encourage tactical positioning.

---

# 7. Running Combat Smoothly

## Keep Descriptions Tactical

Do not describe zones geometrically.

Instead:
> “The courtyard is exposed and under machine-gun observation.”

Not:
> “The courtyard is 40 meters wide.”

---

## Reiterate Tactical Information Often

Players should always understand:
- where danger exists,
- where cover exists,
- and what movement options are available.

---

## Let Zones Evolve

Good battlefields change.

Examples:
- a vehicle explodes,
- smoke fills a corridor,
- psychic backlash ignites a chamber,
- a wall collapses,
- reinforcements breach a new entry point.

Add or modify traits during combat.

---

# 8. Common Failure Modes

## Too Many Zones
Creates hidden grid combat.

## Meaningless Zones
If zones lack tactical identity, players stop caring about movement.

## Excessive Precision
Do not secretly calculate meters behind the scenes.

## Static Battlefields
Zones should evolve under pressure.

---

# 9. Practical PsiOps Example

## Black Site Breach

```text
             [Observation Roof]
                      |
[Drainage Canal] — [Motor Pool] — [Main Entrance]
                      |
             [Containment Wing]
```

### Observation Roof
- Elevated
- Exposed
- Excellent sightlines

### Motor Pool
- Vehicle cover
- Burning wreckage
- Open lanes of fire

### Main Entrance
- Chokepoint
- Defensive barricades

### Containment Wing
- Psychic distortion
- Hazardous anomalies
- Tight interior fighting

### Drainage Canal
- Concealed approach
- Difficult terrain

Immediately, tactical choices emerge:
- flank through the canal,
- suppress the roof,
- breach directly,
- or push into the unstable containment sector before reinforcements arrive.
