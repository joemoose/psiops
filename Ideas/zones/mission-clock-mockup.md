Two things to note about this mock-up as a rulebook diagram:

The **color distinction** between obstacle (teal) and threat (red) does a lot of work — it encodes not just progress state but emotional valence. Obstacle clocks are things you want to fill; threat clocks are things filling against you. The colors make that legible at a glance without needing prose to say it.

The **segment dividers** come from the white stroke between each path rather than separate line elements, which keeps the SVG clean while making each individual segment countable.

A few production decisions you'd want to make before this goes into the manual:

- **Segment count labeling** — the "6 segments / 4 of 6 filled" subtitle is explanatory text for the diagram itself, not rulebook copy. In the actual layout you'd strip that and let the chapter text carry it.
- **Third size** — the rules mention 4, 6, and 8 segments. Depending on page real estate, you might want a third example (8-seg, partially filled) shown at a smaller scale alongside these two, so players can see all three standard configurations.
- **Numbering** — adding segment index numbers (1–6 around the ring) would help players track progress verbally at the table ("we're at segment 3 of 6"), which a purely visual clock doesn't support.
