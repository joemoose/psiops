# Tactical Zones
### PsiOps Field Manual Excerpt — Spatial Doctrine for Operators

PsiOps does not track combat in meters, squares, or miniature-scale movement. Tactical combat is handled through **zones**: meaningful areas of the battlefield defined by position, exposure, cover, and tactical purpose.

A zone is not an exact measurement. It is a place that matters.

Examples:
- a rooftop firing position,
- a burning hallway,
- a street under sniper observation,
- a laboratory full of unstable psychic equipment.

The question is never:

> “Exactly where are you standing?”

The question is:

> “What tactical position are you holding?”

---

## Operational Distance vs Tactical Zones

PsiOps uses two scales of distance.

### Operational Distance
Outside structured combat, distance is narrative and approximate.

The GM describes situations in practical terms:
> “The bunker is a short sprint across open ground.”

No measurements are required unless the fiction specifically demands them.

This mode handles:
- infiltration,
- maneuver,
- stealth,
- investigation,
- and general scene flow.

---

### Tactical Zones
When combat begins and exact positioning matters, the scene shifts into **Tactical Zones**.

The battlefield is divided into a handful of important areas connected together.

Example:

```text
                [Roof]
                   |
[Alley] — [Street] — [Lobby]
                   |
             [Parking Lot]
```

Each zone has:
- tactical advantages,
- hazards,
- cover,
- or movement constraints.

If a zone has no meaningful tactical identity, it should not exist separately.

---

## Movement

Moving between connected zones usually costs **one action**.

Special conditions may alter this:
- rubble or mud may require extra effort,
- crossing open ground may expose you to fire,
- smoke or psychic distortion may hinder movement,
- some transitions may be effectively free.

---

## Range

Weapon ranges are determined by **zone distance**, not measurements.

| Distance | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Engaged | Same zone |
| Nearby | 1 zone away |
| Far | 2 zones away |
| Distant | 3 zones away |
| Extreme | 4+ zones away |

Different weapons perform better or worse at different zone distances.

A sniper rifle dominates distant sightlines.  
A shotgun is devastating inside the same zone.  
An assault rifle excels across connected positions.

---

## Zone Traits

Zones define the tactical situation.

| Zone Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Cover Position | Sandbags, wrecked vehicles |
| Exposure Area | Streets, courtyards |
| Interior Space | Offices, tunnels |
| Elevated Position | Rooftops, balconies |
| Chokepoint | Doorways, stairwells |
| Concealed Area | Woods, drainage ditches |
| Tactical Objective | Generator room, ritual chamber |

The battlefield matters because the environment matters.

Operators survive by controlling terrain, not counting squares.
