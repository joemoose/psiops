    Issue 1.4 — Stress and Fatigue chapter follows Equipment¶

    Location: # Stress and Fatigue (after Equipment)

    The Combat chapter already introduces both stress (in the Injuries and Dying table) and fatigue (explicitly, as inventory items acquired from the dying table). A player reading Combat will encounter "Take 2 units of fatigue" and "Take 2 points of stress" without the rules chapter that explains what either term means at scale. Then the Equipment chapter discusses inventory, and only afterward does the Stress and Fatigue chapter appear.

    Recommendation: Move # Stress and Fatigue to immediately follow # Field Operations (or even precede # Combat). Fatigue interacts directly with inventory — consider whether the fatigue rules belong in Equipment or in a dedicated section that precedes Combat.

    Issue 6.1 — Mission Clocks requires a diagram (already flagged as a placeholder)¶

    See Issue 5.3. This is the single place in the document where a visual is both explicitly requested and would most materially improve comprehension. The prose description of "segments marked off" is abstract without a visual.

    Issue 6.2 — Range bands would benefit from a distance reference¶

    Location: # Combat > ## Range Bands (lines 519–524)

    The four bands are defined in prose, with approximate distances (2m / sidearm range / rifle range / heavy-weapon range). These ranges interact with movement costs and weapon range modifiers in the following tables. A horizontal bar diagram — even a simple text-art ruler showing Close / Nearby / Far / Distant with approximate meter markers — would make this section a useful play reference rather than a passage to re-read during combat.

    Recommendation: Add a small range band diagram below the prose definitions. Consider adding a "typical meters" column to the range bands prose block.

    Issue 6.5 — Character creation would benefit from a checklist table¶

    Location: # Recruitment & Assignment > ## Fielding an Operator

    The ten-step list is clear but linear. During a session-zero character creation session, players must scroll up and down across the attributes, core drives, neural weapons, and specializations tables while simultaneously tracking which steps they've completed.

    Recommendation: Add a two-column reference table at the beginning of the section:

    Step Task Where to Record
    1 Assign attribute scores Attributes block
    2 Roll core drive Core drive field
    … … …

    This doesn't replace the prose steps but gives players a scannable checklist during play.

    Issue 6.6 — Attribute table examples are player-voice dialogue, not mechanical illustrations¶

    Location: # Field Operations > ## How to Make an Action Check > ### Choose an Applicable Attribute (lines 256–261)

    The "Example" column shows player dialogue ("I kick down the door…") rather than illustrating when the GM would call for each attribute. The examples are helpful for flavor but don't address common edge cases a new player will hit, such as: "Which attribute do I use for a neural weapon?" (WILLPOWER, but it's only mentioned in the WILLPOWER row's description), or "What do I roll to intimidate someone?" (potentially STRENGTH or WILLPOWER depending on framing — which the current table doesn't address).

    Recommendation: Add a "Common Uses" row or expand the example column to include at least one example of an edge case per attribute, particularly clarifying WILLPOWER's role in neural weapon deployment.
