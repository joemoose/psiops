# Equipment

The equipment you carry into the field is divided into five categories:

- Basic kit
- Tactical supplies
- Combat weapons
- Personal protective system
- Mission-specific equipment

Details on each category follow. 

## Inventory

To carry your equipment, you have **12** inventory slots: **two** in your hands, **four** on your body, and **six** in a rucksack. 

Most items and your basic kit occupy **one** inventory slot. Smaller items, such as a combat knife, may not require a slot. Inventory slots are abstract and rearrangeable.
- Some items might be bundled with multiple units in one inventory slot, such as grenades. If only one of the bundled items is carried, it still requires **1** slot. 
- Bulky two-handed items take up **two** slots.
- Ballistic armor takes up **1—3** slots.
- Unwieldy or heavy items you can't lift alone usually can't be stored in your inventory. Other transport methods may be required.

During a combat round, retrieving an item from your rucksack requires an action. Using an item in a body slot is a free action if you have the required number of open hands. Otherwise, an item in a hand must be dropped as a free action or stowed as an action. 

## Basic Kit

In the game, the *basic kit* is an abstract container for standard-issue gear. It's stowed in your rucksack and always occupies 1 inventory slot, regardless of how many items it contains. Items inside the basic kit don't take up additional slots.

The basic kit exists so you don't have to itemize every piece of utility gear before a mission — it's ready when you need it.

When you need an item from the kit, declare it and retrieve it. No inventory slot is required. Your combat fatigues, boots, and other standard-issue clothing are assumed to be worn. If any basic kit items are lost or destroyed due to operational circumstances, they're gone. The kit doesn't restore them.

Items not originally part of your basic kit, such as mission-specific gear, small arms, or recovered equipment, cannot be stored in it.

## Tactical Supplies

Tactical supplies are smaller items of tactical equipment, such as climbing gear, flashlights, and ammunition. You start each mission with **2** units of tactical supplies.

When you need a piece of gear you aren’t already carrying, request it. If the GM agrees the requested item qualifies as a tactical supply (see the following examples), remove one *tactical supply* from your inventory and add the requested item. 

Tactical supplies let you avoid deciding in advance which additional tactical gear to pack beyond your basic kit. Equipment classified as tactical supplies can, of course, be added to your inventory separately and carried on the mission. Spent tactical supplies can be restocked at a *rendezvous point* during the mission. See [Rest and Recovery](combat.md#rest-and-recovery).

Examples of tactical supplies include:

- Ammunition
- Binoculars
- Climbing gear
- Food and water
- Gun-mounted flashlight
- Infiltration clothing
- Night vision goggles
- Smoke grenades
- Wire cutters
- Zip-ties

Items that aren’t classified as tactical supplies are heavier, more complex, lethal, or highly mission‑specific. Examples include:

- Acetylene cutting torch
- Breaching ram
- Backpack-mounted SATCOM terminal
- Closed‑circuit rebreather
- Explosives
- Heavy weapons
- Ram‑air parachute rig
- Small arms
- Support weapons

## Personal Protective System

Your personal protective system consists of two parts: ballistic body armor and secondary armor, such as a helmet or a riot shield. You can equip as much or as little armor as you like. The heavier your protective system, the less equipment and fewer weapons you can carry. 

### Ballistic Armor

Ballistic body armor is the foundation of your protective system. It consists of soft panels and hard plates designed to stop or slow projectiles and fragments. Three tiers of ballistic body armor are available, each offering greater protection at the cost of increased weight and bulk. 

**Armor Rating** 

Each tier carries an *armor rating* of **1**, **2**, or **3**. When you're hit by an attack, subtract your armor rating from the damage taken.

**Inventory Slots**

Each tier occupies a number of *inventory slots* equal to its armor rating.

**Summary**

| Ballistic Armor | Armor Rating | Inventory Slots |
| :- | :-: | :-: |
| Light (soft panels) | 1 | 1 |
| Medium (soft + plates) | 2 | 2 |
| Heavy (full plate system) | 3 | 3 |

!!! warning

	Some forms of attack damage, including intense flames and most types of neural weapons, are **not reduced** by ballistic armor. 

### Secondary Armor

You can equip *secondary armor*, such as a helmet or a riot shield, for additional protection. Secondary armor provides an *armor rating bonus* of **+1**. Add the bonus to your ballistic body armor’s rating to determine the total points of damage deducted.

The **maximum** amount of damage reduced by your personal protective system is capped at **3 points**, regardless of the total rating after secondary armor bonuses are added.

Each piece of secondary armor requires **1** *inventory slot*. The riot shield must be held in your hand to be effective. A riot shield reduces certain types of attack damage that would otherwise bypass your ballistic armor, such as intense flames.

### Additional Benefits

In addition to an armor rating bonus, secondary protection provides the following effects:

 - **Helmets**: A helmet provides *advantage* on *action rolls* to avoid losing consciousness and on action rolls to avoid *blast* damage, such as from a grenade explosion.
 - **Riot Shield**: If you have a riot shield equipped, you can choose to destroy it to prevent **all** damage you’d otherwise take from an attack. Like a helmet, a riot shield also provides *advantage* on *action rolls* to avoid *blast* damage.

Recall that *advantages* do not stack. If both a helmet and a riot shield are equipped, you gain only **one** advantage to avoid *blast* damage.

### Tradeoffs

Using secondary armor imposes tactical trade-offs. The GM may apply *disadvantage* to *action rolls* involving perception, quick reactions, or stealth. Examples include locating an enemy sniper, acting before your opponents in the first round of combat, or moving without being heard. 

!!! note

	Ballistic body armor does not incur any of these tradeoffs.

## Squad Radios 

Understanding the operational limitations of Cold War-era radios could become critical to your missions. 

Handset squad-coordination radios were primitive by modern standards. Terrain, vegetation, and urban settings killed both signal quality and range. Fast movement interrupted communication. Suggested ranges for the game are as follows.

| Situation | Limitations |
| :- | :- |
| Outdoor coordination | ~500 m–3 km reliably, heavy vegetation cuts range in half |
| Urban | 100s of meters, intermittent failures |
| Long-range comms| Requires backpack-mounted PRC or SATCOM equipment |

## Example: Stowing a Loadout

An example inventory manifest follows. 

*An operator loads up their equipment before the mission. They are packing:*

- Basic kit
- 2 Units of tactical supplies
- Sidearm
- Rifle
- Combat knife
- Tier-1 ballistic body armor (1 armor rating,1 slot)
- Riot shield
- Breaching charge
- Extra ammunition (GM rules that it’s abstract and could restock either the sidearm or the rifle, and takes up a slot.)

Their loadout is arranged as follows:

| Item | Location | Slots | Notes |
| :-- | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Riot shield | Hand | 1 | — |
| Sidearm | Hand | 1 | — |
| Ballistic armor | Body | 1 | — |
| Combat knife | Body | 0 | GM rules that it’s equipped on the body but does not require a slot |
| Rifle | Body | 2 | — |
| Basic kit | Rucksack | 1 | — |
| 2 Tactical supplies | Rucksack | 2 | — |
| Breaching charge | Rucksack | 1 | — |
| Ammo | Rucksack | 1  | — |

The operator has 0 hand slots remaining, 1 open body slot, and 1 open rucksack slot. 

!!! note

	In this example, a helmet is not equipped. If the operator were to take one on the mission, it would occupy the remaining body slot.
