# Your Character

## Introduction

You're the kind of soldier they don't write about. Shadow ops. Off-ledger. Neural weapons. The Cold War has a hidden front, and you're on it.

You've seen things that don't fit a debrief. You can do things that don't fit a chain of command. The threats you'll face might bleed, but they shouldn't exist.

You won't be fighting alone. You won't be fighting safe.

Welcome to the unit, operator. 

## Creating an Operative

To create your new character, follow the steps below.

1. Assign the scores **2**, **1**, **0**, and **-1** to your four attributes — **STRENGTH**, **REFLEXES**, **TACTICS**, and **WILLPOWER** — in any order you prefer.
2. Roll a **D66** to select your character's **Core Drive** from the corresponding table below. What's a **D66**? Roll a **D6** twice, but don't add the rolls; instead, read the results in order. For example, if you roll a 2 first and then a 4, that's a 24.
	- **Note**: Each character must have a unique drive. Reroll any duplicates.
3. Roll a **D66** twice to select two starting **Neural Weapons** from the corresponding table below.
4. Roll a **D66** twice to select two starting **Advanced Specializations** from the corresponding table below.
5. Set your maximum **Hit Points (HP)** to 6 and your starting **Momentum Points** to 2.
6. In addition to the standard loadout listed on your character sheet, add **2 items of mission-specific equipment** of your choice to your inventory. See the equipment lists below for examples.
7. Set your **armor rating** to **2**, the cumulative rating from your equipped armor.
8. Choose your **native language** and a **second language** you're fluent in.
	- Choose additional languages equal to the higher of WILLPOWER and TACTICS. If both are zero or lower, choose no additional languages.
9. Choose your character's **codename**.

Your operative is ready. Everything that follows tells you how the operation runs — mechanics, combat, contingencies. Read them. Know them.

Weapons free, soldier. Oscar Mike.

## Inventory

Characters have **12 inventory slots**: two held in their hands, four on their body (such as weapons or equipped armor), and a backpack with six slots. Retrieving an item from a backpack requires spending an action in a combat round.

Most items take up one slot, and small items may be bundled, per the GM. Slots are abstract and can be rearranged at the GM's discretion.
- Bulky items that require two hands to carry take up **two slots**.
- Unwieldy and heavy items that one person can't generally lift can't be placed in your inventory. Other means of transport must be arranged.

## Tactical Supplies

Tactical supplies are a set of small, mission-related, standard-issue military gear, including rope, flashlights, multitools, and ammunition. You generally start each mission with **3** units of tactical supplies. Spent supplies are restocked at base between missions. 

### Usage

Tactical supplies let you avoid deciding in advance what gear to pack. When you need a specific piece of gear on a mission, declare what you need, and the GM determines whether the item qualifies as a standard tactical supply. If the GM approves, mark off one *tactical supply* from your inventory, and you have that item. 

### Examples

Examples of gear categorized as standard Tactical Supplies:

- Ammunition
- Bandages
- Food and water
- Gun-mounted flashlight
- Night-vision device
- Rope
- Sleeping bag
- Walkie-talkie
- Wire cutters
- Zip-ties

Examples of gear **not** categorized as standard Tactical Supplies:

- Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs)
- Explosives
- Firearms
- Heavy weapons
- Parachute
- Satellite transceiver
- SCUBA gear and air tank
- Vehicles

## Character Advancement

Characters advance when they act in accordance with their *drive*. For example, a *crusader* advances if they act ethically during their mission.

### New Drives

If your current *drive* feels played out for your character, you can choose a new one after advancing at least once in your current *drive* and returning to base after a mission. 

### Character Improvement

After your character advances and you return to base, choose **one** of the following improvements: 

- Add **1 point** to an *attribute score*, up to a maximum of **3**.
- Add **1D3** to your *maximum HP*. There is no upper limit. 
- Roll **D66** to randomly select a new *neural weapon* from the Neural Weapons table.
- Choose a new *advanced skill* from the Advanced Skills table. The new skill should relate to meaningful actions you attempted during the previous mission, whether successful or not.
